This paper examines which economic and governance conditions make data-sharing ecosystems sustainable beyond pilot projects and public subsidies. Based on interviews, workshops, and conference feedback, it reframes data-sharing ecosystems as generative, use-case-oriented ecosystems rather than data marketplaces.

The analysis identifies six conditions: upfront and recurrent investment, use-case-based innovation, integration complexity, security risk, competitive risk, and incentive alignment. It also shows how strategic and technical orchestration must adapt across emergence, diffusion, and diversification phases.
